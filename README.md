ABSTRACT
We present a comprehensive atlas of human esophageal development, integrating single-cell transcriptomics and spatial profiling to identify stage-specific epithelial and mesenchymal populations. Using this framework, we developed a scalable 3D esophagoid model that recapitulates key features of esophageal architecture, including basal-to-luminal stratification and that maintains stromal interactions for robust esophagoid formation. Functional analysis of patient esophagus tissue with WNT2B mutations and in vitro esophagoid analyses reveal that WNT signaling regulates epithelial progenitor dynamics by repressing self-renewal of TP63⁺ basal cells and inhibiting proliferation, thereby shaping epithelial organization. Together, these findings define the esophagoid as a physiologically relevant platform for studying epithelial-stromal interactions and modeling esophageal development and disease.
